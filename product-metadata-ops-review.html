Product Information in Silq

The Product Library — our master catalog

The Product Library is Silq’s central repository: every product family and variation we maintain for a client lives here before it is used on any project or shipment.

📚

What the Product Library is

Think of it as the source of truth for product information at the organization level. When Ops uploads a catalog spreadsheet or adds a product manually, it is stored in the library first. Projects and shipments then pull from this library — they do not replace it.

Layer 1 · Product families Styles shared across the catalog — name, category, HTS, origin, image

How we add to the library

Catalog spreadsheet upload (TSV) is the main bulk path. Products can also be added or edited manually in Silq.

How we use the library

When planning a project, search the library and pick products & variations to add. Search works across name, SKU, HTS, color, and size.

♻️
How it stays consistent

One library entry is reused across many projects. Updating the catalog updates the master record; project order lines keep their own qty & price.

Key distinction: The library holds what the product is (identity, classification, SKU attributes). Projects and shipments hold what happened (how many ordered, how many shipped, PO numbers, inspection on that project).

Product family vs variations

Every item in the Product Library sits in one of two buckets: a product family (the shared style) or a variation (one specific sellable version). Here is how to tell them apart — with examples.

Product family

The shared style

“What is this product line?”

  • One record per style / product line
  • Same name, HTS, category for all versions
  • Holds details that don’t change by color or size
  • Often called the “parent” or “style”
Variation / SKU

One sellable version

“Which exact item are we shipping?”

  • Many variations under one family
  • Each color / size combo is its own row
  • Holds details that can differ between versions
  • What you pick when adding qty to a project

💡
Simple rule: If two items share the same product name and HTS but differ by color, size, fabric, weight, or price — they are variations of the same product family. In the catalog spreadsheet, the product row is the family; each color/size line is a variation.
When entering data — ask yourself: “Would this change if we switched from Navy to Charcoal, or from M to L?” If yes → put it on the variation. If no → put it on the product family.

The four layers of product information

Silq stores product details in four levels — from the broadest (the whole style) down to the most specific (what ships on one shipment). Layers 1 & 2 live in the Product Library; layers 3 & 4 are created when products are added to projects and shipments.

🎯
Think of it like zooming in on a map. Layer 1 is the whole product line. Layer 2 is one SKU. Layer 3 is what was ordered on a project. Layer 4 is what actually leaves on a specific shipment. Details get more specific as you go down.

Where you see each view in Silq

Summary view — by product family
  • Project product list (one card per style)
  • Product detail page — tasks, inspections, documents
  • Product catalog when browsing the library
  • Cost & analytics reports (rolled-up totals)
Detail view — by variation
  • Adding or editing products on a project
  • Shipment products tab (each SKU + shipped qty)
  • Catalog spreadsheet upload (one row per variation)
  • Checking a specific color/size qty or price
Quick tip: If you only see a total quantity and no breakdown by color or size, you are in the summary view. Open the product on the project or check the shipment products tab to see the detail view with each SKU listed separately.

What information we store at each layer

Grouped by business topic — not every product has every field filled in. See Summary view vs detail view for how fields appear on screen.

Identity & naming

  • Product name and description
  • Client style / external ID
  • SKU or variation ID
  • Variation display name
  • Product image
  • Brand (via legacy product groups)

Classification

  • Product category (e.g. Apparel)
  • Product type (e.g. Knitwear)
  • HTS / tariff code
  • Country of origin

Variation attributes (apparel-focused)

  • Color
  • Size
  • Fit type
  • Pattern type
  • Fabric / material (free text)
  • Construction details
  • Artwork reference

Commercial & physical

  • Unit price (FOB in USD)
  • Net weight per unit
  • Gross weight per unit
  • Volume per unit (CBM)
  • PO quantity
  • Shipped quantity

Order & shipment context

  • Which SILQ project
  • Which shipment
  • PO number (when linked)
  • Order-specific HTS override
  • Source of the data (manual, upload, etc.)

Inspection results (on project)

  • Inspection type and date
  • Pass / Fail / Hold result
  • Order quantity from report
  • Ex-factory date
  • Summary for client-facing updates

Where product information comes from

Not everything is typed in by hand. Here is how data typically enters Silq today.

Source What it populates How it works
Product catalog spreadsheet
Bulk upload		 Product family, SKU details, and optionally PO qty & shipped qty when a SILQ project or shipment ID is included Ops or client uploads a TSV file. Silq validates HTS codes, colors, and sizes, then creates or updates products.
Manual entry in Silq
Manual		 Any product, order, or shipment field Team members add or edit products directly on projects and shipments.
Inspection reports
Auto-read		 Inspection result summary on the project (pass/fail, dates, quantities) When an inspection report is uploaded, Silq reads the document and stores key results — it does not change the core product catalog (name, HTS, weights, etc.).
Accepted quote / demo project copy
Manual trigger		 Products and order details copied from a template or previous project Used when setting up demo or cloned projects from an accepted quote.
Legacy purchase orders
Being phased out		 PO numbers linked to products (historical data) Older PO system was migrated into the current model. New PO numbers are added manually or via spreadsheet — there is no PO PDF parser today.
Tech packs
Not parsed		 Stored as documents only Tech packs attach to workflow milestones but their contents are not automatically pulled into product fields.
Customs entry (7501), commercial invoice, bills of lading
Shipment-level		 Used for customs validation and shipment totals — not for building the product catalog These documents support clearance and shipment records. HTS on a 7501 can be checked against what is on the shipment, but the catalog is not updated from them.
Important for Ops: The catalog spreadsheet is today’s main bulk-ingest path. If a field is not in that template and not entered manually, Silq probably does not have it.

Where we use this information

Product data is not just stored — it powers multiple workflows across Silq.

📦
Shipment products tab
Shows what is on each shipment — quantities, prices, weights — for ops and client visibility.
📋
Project product lists
All products on a project with PO quantities, milestones, and inspection links.
🔍
Inspections
Links inspection reports to specific products; stores parsed pass/fail and summary on the project.
🛃
Customs clearance
HTS codes and product descriptions on shipments are checked against customs entry documents.
💰
Cost & analytics reports
Per-unit FOB, shipping, customs, and inspection costs rolled up for insights and client reporting.
🔎
Product search
Find products across the org by name, SKU, category, color, size, HTS, and origin.
✅
Milestones & tasks
Adding a product to a project can automatically create the right workflow tasks.
📊
Project overview
High-level view of product progress, quantities, and status across the project lifecycle.

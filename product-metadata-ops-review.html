Overview — why this matters

Every shipment in Silq is tied to product details: what it is, how much it weighs, what it costs, where it was made, and how it should be classified for customs. Getting this right affects quoting, inspections, booking freight, customs clearance, client reporting, and audit trails.

Today, product information lives in four connected layers — from the general product family down to the specific shipment line. Some details are shared across all variations; others change per order or per shipment.

Simple example: “Men’s knitted cardigan” is the product family. “Navy · Size M · SKU VOL2838” is one specific variation. The PO might say 500 units at $8.75 each. Shipment 1 might ship 120 of those units.

The four layers of product information

Think of these as nesting levels — each layer adds context for a specific project or shipment.

Layer 1
Product family (master product)

The style or product line shared across all colors and sizes. Example: “Men’s knitted cardigan” under client Acme Apparel. Holds defaults like category, general HTS code, and country of origin.

Layer 2
Product variation (SKU)

One sellable unit — a specific color, size, and identifier. Example: Navy · Size M · VOL2838. Holds weights, volume, unit price, fabric, fit, and artwork details that differ by SKU.

Layer 3
Order / PO details

What was ordered on a specific project or purchase order — quantity, FOB price, weights, and any order-specific overrides (e.g. a different HTS code for this PO only).

Layer 4
Shipment details

What actually ships on a given shipment — shipped quantity, shipping-side price and weights. This is what shows on the shipment products tab and feeds into cost and customs workflows.

Product family → SKU / variation → Order on project → Shipment line

What information we store at each layer

Grouped by business topic — not every product has every field filled in.

Identity & naming

  • Product name and description
  • Client style / external ID
  • SKU or variation ID
  • Variation display name
  • Product image
  • Brand (via legacy product groups)

Classification

  • Product category (e.g. Apparel)
  • Product type (e.g. Knitwear)
  • HTS / tariff code
  • Country of origin

Variation attributes (apparel-focused)

  • Color
  • Size
  • Fit type
  • Pattern type
  • Fabric / material (free text)
  • Construction details
  • Artwork reference

Commercial & physical

  • Unit price (FOB in USD)
  • Net weight per unit
  • Gross weight per unit
  • Volume per unit (CBM)
  • PO quantity
  • Shipped quantity

Order & shipment context

  • Which SILQ project
  • Which shipment
  • PO number (when linked)
  • Order-specific HTS override
  • Source of the data (manual, upload, etc.)

Inspection results (on project)

  • Inspection type and date
  • Pass / Fail / Hold result
  • Order quantity from report
  • Ex-factory date
  • Summary for client-facing updates

Where product information comes from

Not everything is typed in by hand. Here is how data typically enters Silq today.

Source What it populates How it works
Product catalog spreadsheet
Bulk upload		 Product family, SKU details, and optionally PO qty & shipped qty when a SILQ project or shipment ID is included Ops or client uploads a TSV file. Silq validates HTS codes, colors, and sizes, then creates or updates products.
Manual entry in Silq
Manual		 Any product, order, or shipment field Team members add or edit products directly on projects and shipments.
Inspection reports
Auto-read		 Inspection result summary on the project (pass/fail, dates, quantities) When an inspection report is uploaded, Silq reads the document and stores key results — it does not change the core product catalog (name, HTS, weights, etc.).
Accepted quote / demo project copy
Manual trigger		 Products and order details copied from a template or previous project Used when setting up demo or cloned projects from an accepted quote.
Legacy purchase orders
Being phased out		 PO numbers linked to products (historical data) Older PO system was migrated into the current model. New PO numbers are added manually or via spreadsheet — there is no PO PDF parser today.
Tech packs
Not parsed		 Stored as documents only Tech packs attach to workflow milestones but their contents are not automatically pulled into product fields.
Customs entry (7501), commercial invoice, bills of lading
Shipment-level		 Used for customs validation and shipment totals — not for building the product catalog These documents support clearance and shipment records. HTS on a 7501 can be checked against what is on the shipment, but the catalog is not updated from them.
Important for Ops: The catalog spreadsheet is today’s main bulk-ingest path. If a field is not in that template and not entered manually, Silq probably does not have it.

Where we use this information

Product data is not just stored — it powers multiple workflows across Silq.

📦
Shipment products tab
Shows what is on each shipment — quantities, prices, weights — for ops and client visibility.
📋
Project product lists
All products on a project with PO quantities, milestones, and inspection links.
🔍
Inspections
Links inspection reports to specific products; stores parsed pass/fail and summary on the project.
🛃
Customs clearance
HTS codes and product descriptions on shipments are checked against customs entry documents.
💰
Cost & analytics reports
Per-unit FOB, shipping, customs, and inspection costs rolled up for insights and client reporting.
🔎
Product search
Find products across the org by name, SKU, category, color, size, HTS, and origin.
✅
Milestones & tasks
Adding a product to a project can automatically create the right workflow tasks.
📊
Project overview
High-level view of product progress, quantities, and status across the project lifecycle.

Known gaps — what we do not capture well today

These came up in our internal review. We want to confirm with Ops which ones actually block your work vs. nice-to-have.

  • HTS and country of origin live mostly at the product-family level. If two SKUs in the same style need different HTS codes or origins, it is awkward today.
  • No package or carton dimensions. We have volume per unit but not length/width/height, carton sizes, or units per carton — information carriers and freight teams often need.
  • No dangerous goods or battery flags. Products with lithium batteries or DG restrictions are not flagged in the catalog.
  • No regulatory flags (CPSC, FDA, etc.). Consumer product or FDA-regulated items are not marked in product data.
  • Marketing description vs. customs description are the same thing. There is no separate “commercial description” safe for customs filings.
  • Shipment product data can change if someone edits the catalog later. We do not currently “freeze” what was on a shipment at booking time for audit purposes.
  • Tech packs and PO PDFs are not read automatically. Details in those documents must be entered manually or via spreadsheet.
  • Apparel-focused variation fields. Color, size, fit, and fabric work well for apparel; other categories (furniture, electronics, food) may need different attributes.
  • Fabric is free text, not structured. Material percentages (e.g. 60% cotton / 40% polyester) are not stored in a structured way for HTS logic.

Possible next steps (for discussion — not committed)

Based on internal planning and your feedback, these are improvements we may prioritize. Nothing here is final until Ops weighs in.

High priority
Clearer SKU identity — separate SKU from other external IDs; support standard barcodes (GTIN/UPC) where clients provide them.
High priority
Customs-ready descriptions — a dedicated commercial description separate from marketing copy, usable on customs filings.
High priority
HTS and origin at SKU level — allow different codes/origins per variation without splitting the product family.
High priority
Shipment snapshot — lock product details on a shipment at booking time so later catalog edits do not change history.
Medium priority
Dimensions & packaging — unit size, packaged size, carton count, units per carton for freight planning.
Medium priority
Compliance flags — battery, dangerous goods, CPSC, FDA, and structured fabric/material percentages for apparel HTS.
Later
Smarter document intake — read more from POs, packing lists, and tech packs instead of manual re-entry (scope TBD after Ops input).
